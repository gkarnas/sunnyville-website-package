Three useful starting points

Choose by what you want the system to do.

01 / Solar

Solar-first home

For households mainly wanting to reduce daytime grid electricity use and make good use of suitable roof space.

  • Solar sized around the home and roof
  • Inverter selected for the system design
  • Future battery compatibility considered
  • Monitoring and system handover

02 / Solar + battery

Store more of your solar

For households wanting to shift excess daytime solar into the evening and reduce how much electricity they buy back later.

  • Solar and battery designed together
  • Battery capacity matched to usable energy
  • Power output checked against household loads
  • Backup options discussed separately

03 / Backup focused

Solar + battery + backup planning

For homes where blackout operation matters. Battery capacity alone does not determine what can run during an outage.

  • Essential or broader backup loads identified
  • Inverter output and surge requirements checked
  • Solar recharge during outages considered
  • Reserve settings discussed

Why no fixed one-size-fits-all bundle?

Bigger is not automatically better.

A package should be a starting point, not a shortcut around proper design. We look at when you use electricity, how much suitable roof area you have, whether you already own solar, and what you expect to add later.

The final quote identifies the actual equipment, system size, warranties and scope for your home.

What we consider

  • Current and future electricity use
  • Roof orientation, usable area and shade
  • Existing inverter and solar equipment
  • Battery capacity versus battery power
  • EV, pool, air-conditioning and electric hot water
  • Backup expectations
  • Network and site requirements
